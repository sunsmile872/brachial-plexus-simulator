🧠 Pre-Ganglionic (Root Avulsion / Radiculopathy) vs Post-Ganglionic (Plexopathy) Discrimination

The Core EMG Paradigm
Diagnostic Parameter Pre-Ganglionic Lesion (Root Level) Post-Ganglionic Lesion (Plexus Level) Physiologic Mechanism (Why?)
Sensory Nerve Action Potential (SNAP) NORMAL / PRESERVED REDUCED or ABSENT Sensory cell bodies reside in the DRG. Pre-ganglionic lesion leaves cell body intact, preventing distal Wallerian degeneration!
Clinical Sensation in Dermatome NUMB / ANESTHETIC NUMB / HYPOESTHETIC Central disconnection causes clinical numbness in both, creating the "Pre-ganglionic Paradox" of normal SNAP with numb skin.
Cervical Paraspinal Needle EMG DENERVATED (Fibs / PSWs) COMPLETELY NORMAL Dorsal primary rami branch off proximal to the plexus. Involvement confirms lesion at the spinal root/nerve level.
Rhomboid & Serratus Anterior DENERVATED (if C5-C7 roots) SPARED (Normal) Dorsal Scapular & Long Thoracic nerves emerge directly from roots before trunk formation.
Horner's Syndrome Frequent in T1 avulsion (sympathetics) Rare (except Pancoast tumor direct spread) T1 ventral root avulsion disrupts sympathetic preganglionic fibers destined for superior cervical ganglion.
Surgical Recovery & Transfer Zero spontaneous recovery; needs urgent nerve transfer (Oberlin, spinal accessory) Potential for graft repair / spontaneous neurotization depending on grade Avulsion from spinal cord cannot regenerate spontaneously back into CNS.

THE BRACHIAL PLEXUS

A CLINICAL NEUROANATOMY & ELECTRODIAGNOSTIC REFERENCE FOR PM&R PHYSICIANS

PM&R Board Guide
Preston & Shapiro • Perotto
📐 1. Core Architectural Architecture (Roots • Trunks • Divisions • Cords • Terminals)
ROOTS (5) • Interscalene Triangle

C5, C6, C7, C8, T1 exit between anterior & middle scalenes over 1st rib.

  • Dorsal Scapular (C5): Rhomboids, Levator scapulae.
  • Long Thoracic (C5-7): Serratus anterior (winging).
  • Phrenic (C3-5): Diaphragm anterior scalene.
TRUNKS (3) • Posterior Triangle

Cross 1st rib behind subclavian artery. Erb's point at upper trunk.

  • Superior (C5-6): Suprascapular (Supraspinatus/Infraspinatus) & N. to Subclavius.
  • Middle (C7): Pure continuation.
  • Inferior (C8-T1): Crosses 1st rib (TOS site).
DIVISIONS (6) • Retroclavicular

Posterior to middle 1/3 of clavicle. No extra collateral branches!

  • 3 Anterior Divisions: Supply flexor compartments (join Lateral & Medial cords).
  • 3 Posterior Divisions: Supply extensor compartments (unite into Posterior cord).
CORDS (3) • Axilla (2nd Part Axillary A.)

Named by position relative to axillary artery under pec minor.

  • Lateral (C5-7): Lateral Pectoral (pec major clavicular).
  • Posterior (C5-T1): Upper & Lower Subscapulars, Thoracodorsal (latissimus).
  • Medial (C8-T1): Medial Pectoral, Med Cut Arm, MABC (C8-T1).
TERMINAL BRANCHES (5) • The "M" Shape

Surround the 3rd part of the axillary artery.

  • Musculocutaneous (C5-7): Biceps, Brachialis; LAC sensory.
  • Axillary (C5-6): Deltoid, Teres minor (quadrangular space).
  • Radial (C5-T1): Triceps, Extensors, Superficial radial.
  • Median (C5-T1): Formed by Lateral + Medial roots.
  • Ulnar (C8-T1): All intrinsics, FCU, FDP 3/4.
⭐ CRITICAL PM&R LANDMARKS
  • MABC SNAP: Gold standard for True TOS vs Ulnar neuropathy.
  • Rhomboid / Serratus: Spared in postganglionic plexopathy; denervated in root avulsion.
  • EIP Muscle (Radial C8): Distinguishes Lower Trunk (abnormal) from Medial Cord (spared!).
⚡ 2. High-Yield Electrodiagnostic & Clinical Syndromes
Condition Anatomical Level Clinical Presentation Diagnostic NCS / EMG Signature
Erb-Duchenne Palsy Superior Trunk (C5-C6) Waiter's Tip: adducted, internally rotated arm, extended elbow, pronated forearm. Absent biceps reflex. Reduced LAC & Superficial Radial SNAP. Denervation in Deltoid, Biceps, Infraspinatus. Sparing of Rhomboid/Paraspinals.
Klumpke Palsy Inferior Trunk (C8-T1) True Claw Hand: weakness of all hand intrinsics, FDP, FPL. Horner's syndrome if T1 sympathetics disrupted. Absent MABC and Ulnar SNAP. Low median/ulnar CMAPs. Denervation in APB, FDI, and Radial C8 (EIP).
True Neurogenic TOS Lower Trunk / C8-T1 band Gilliatt-Sumner Hand: severe thenar (APB) atrophy > hypothenar, aching medial forearm pain. Absent MABC SNAP (most sensitive!). Attenuated median APB CMAP. Ulnar SNAP normal or mild.
Radiation Plexopathy Superior Trunk (usually) Painless weakness, paresthesias, lymphedema, years after axillary/breast radiation. ⭐ MYOKYMIC DISCHARGES (grouped 20-70 Hz spontaneous firing) on needle EMG.
Pre-Ganglionic Root Avulsion Roots C5-T1 avulsed from cord Flail, anesthetic limb, Horner's syndrome, severe neuropathic pain. Zero spontaneous recovery. ⭐ NORMAL SNAPs despite complete sensory anesthesia (Pre-ganglionic Paradox!). 4+ paraspinal denervation.
⚠️ 3. Needle EMG Landmark & Safety Matrix (Perotto Guide)
Serratus Anterior (C5-C7)

Landmark: Mid-axillary line over 5th/6th rib, lateral to inferior scapular angle.

High Pneumothorax Risk: Angle needle strictly tangential onto the rib bone floor; NEVER enter intercostal spaces!

Rhomboids (C5 Root)

Landmark: Midway between spine and inferior angle of scapula, medial to vertebral border.

Pneumothorax Danger: Traverse trapezius with needle directed towards scapula, not deep into thorax.

Extensor Indicis Proprius (C7-C8)

Landmark: 2 fingerbreadths proximal to ulnar styloid on dorsal forearm, radial to ulna.

Safe Landmark: Key radial C8 muscle that separates Lower Trunk from Medial Cord.
